Zcash NU7 cuts target block time to 25 seconds

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, cutting target blocks to 25 seconds while limiting shielded activity and redirecting most transaction fees to a reserve.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 8, 2026 · 21:15 UTC3 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 network upgrade at block 3,543,405 on Nov. 5, cutting the target interval between blocks from 75 seconds to 25 seconds. The change is intended to shorten the wait for a first confirmation on both shielded and transparent payments. CoinDesk’s report on NU7’s activation said blocks initially averaged about 39 seconds as mining difficulty adjusted; the target is not a guarantee that every payment will confirm in 25 seconds.

How much faster should a Zcash payment confirm?

The target block interval is now one-third of its former length, so the expected wait for a first confirmation falls from about 75 seconds to about 25, according to the Zcash Foundation’s Zebra 7.0.0 release notes. A service that waits for three confirmations could see its expected wait fall from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds. Each exchange, merchant or other service sets its own confirmation requirements, however, so the network change alone does not determine when funds become usable.

Shorter intervals leave miners less time to receive and verify new blocks before competing work appears. CoinDesk reported that a test of 25-second blocks across 80 computers in 11 regions discarded 3.43% of blocks, compared with 1.57% at the previous 75-second pace. Developers also changed transaction checks so nodes can retain some work done when a transaction first arrives, while still rechecking details that depend on current balances and chain history. The early mainnet average of roughly 39 seconds suggests the new target was still settling in after activation.

What changes for shielded payments and wallet operators?

NU7 adds per-block limits on activity in Zcash’s shielded pools: Orchard, Ironwood and Sapling. The Foundation says the limits raise Orchard’s estimated capacity from 2.9 to 6.6 transactions per second while reducing worst-case bandwidth that spam activity could impose on light wallets by about 37%. The mechanism addresses a cost of private payments: wallets scan the chain to find transactions meant for them, so more shielded activity can mean more data to download and process.

Those figures describe protocol capacity and worst-case sync load, not a guarantee of everyday throughput or wallet performance. The Foundation says Zebra applies the limits both when assembling block templates and when accepting transactions into its mempool, rejecting oversized activity earlier. Node operators also had to upgrade compatible software to remain on the consensus chain after activation.

Who pays for the faster network, and what should users watch?

NU7 also changes how transaction fees are allocated. Miners keep 40% of each block’s fees; the remaining 60% goes into a reserve intended to support future block rewards. The reserve is scheduled to begin gradual reissuance at block 8,939,664, expected in 2031. The Foundation says block subsidies were reduced in proportion to the faster block schedule, leaving daily issuance, the four-year halving schedule and the 21 million ZEC cap unchanged.

There is a direct cost to one group of users: NU7 disallows version 4 transactions, the format used by Zcash’s original Sprout pool. The Foundation says ZEC remaining there becomes unspendable; CoinDesk put the amount at about 12,224 ZEC and reported no approved recovery plan as of Nov. 6.

The next signals are whether block intervals settle near 25 seconds, whether discarded blocks remain manageable as activity grows, and whether shielded capacity improves without raising wallet sync burdens. Payment services’ confirmation policies will also determine how much of the shorter target translates into faster access for users.
