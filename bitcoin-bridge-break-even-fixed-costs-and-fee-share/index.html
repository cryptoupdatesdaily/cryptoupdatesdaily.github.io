Bitcoin bridge break-even: fixed costs and fee share

The minimum amount of bitcoin worth bridging depends on fixed costs and your fee limit: divide those costs by the fee room left after percentage charges.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 04:33 UTC4 min read

The smallest worthwhile Bitcoin bridge amount is the point where fixed costs divided by transfer value, plus percentage fees, fall below your chosen cost share. Fixed costs stay roughly constant as the transfer grows, so a protocol minimum sets an allowed floor, not an economic threshold. That threshold rises when the destination transaction is more expensive or you plan to unwrap back to Bitcoin.

How to calculate your bridge break-even

For a route with $10 in fixed costs, 0.25% in percentage fees and a 1% cost limit, the minimum is about $1,333. The calculation is A = F / (target % − p): $10 / (1% − 0.25%) = $1,333. Below that amount, the fixed $10 takes up too much of the transfer’s value to meet the chosen limit.

Here, F is the dollar value of fixed costs, p is the percentage fees, and the target is the maximum total cost as a share of the amount bridged. The target must be greater than p; otherwise, no transfer size can bring the total below the limit. The target is your own decision, based on what you consider worth paying for the route.

Bitcoin miner fees are a fixed cost in this calculation: they depend on transaction virtual size and the fee rate, not on how much BTC the transaction sends, as Bitcoin.org’s fee explanation describes. A small transfer can therefore carry the same miner fee as a larger one when the transactions are the same size. Include destination gas for the mint or swap as another fixed cost, using the fee estimate for the chain and action you plan to take.

Percentage costs include protocol and Locker fees. A swap can add slippage, which also scales with the amount exchanged, so include it in p when you compare the total cost with your target. TeleSwap is one concrete case: its teleswap cross-chain swap uses on-chain transaction verification and collateralized Lockers, which makes the distinction between network costs and Locker or protocol charges useful when assessing a quote.

For a return trip, add the later unwrap costs too. That means including the destination gas for the unwrap and the Bitcoin network fee for the payout, along with any percentage charges on that leg. A one-way bridge and a round trip can have different break-even amounts, even when the initial deposit is identical.

Why the destination chain changes the answer

Take a $1,000 transfer with the same 0.25% percentage fees and the same 1% target. If Ethereum gas makes total fixed costs $10, the fee share is $10 / $1,000 + 0.25% = 1.25%, above the limit. If Polygon gas instead makes total fixed costs $2, the share is $2 / $1,000 + 0.25% = 0.45%, within it. These figures are illustrative route totals, not live fee quotes; the comparison holds the Bitcoin miner fee and percentage charges constant and changes destination gas alone.

The same amount can therefore be worth bridging to Polygon or BNB Smart Chain but not to Ethereum, depending on the actual gas cost of the required transaction. A cheaper destination lowers F and the break-even amount; a more expensive one raises both. Compare the gas estimate for the action you need, rather than treating a chain name as a standing guarantee of lower costs.

For TeleSwap, check the quote’s network, protocol and Locker charges against the amount you intend to move, then add any return-leg costs if you will unwrap. Fee components can change the calculation; a quote gives you the inputs, while the formula tells you whether they fit your threshold.

Related

Do bridges have a minimum deposit amount?

Bridges may set a protocol minimum, but that amount only tells you whether a deposit is accepted. It does not tell you whether the transaction is economical. Apply the break-even formula to the actual fixed and percentage fees to find the smallest amount that meets your cost limit.

Is it cheaper to bridge once with a larger amount than several times?

Usually, one larger transfer spreads each transaction’s fixed costs across more value. Several deposits repeat Bitcoin miner fees and destination gas, so their combined fixed cost can be higher. Compare the total fees for the full route, including any swap and eventual unwrap, before splitting a transfer.

Before sending, watch the estimated Bitcoin fee rate, destination gas, quoted percentage charges, and whether you will need to return the BTC to Bitcoin. Those inputs determine the threshold; the protocol minimum is only the first check.
