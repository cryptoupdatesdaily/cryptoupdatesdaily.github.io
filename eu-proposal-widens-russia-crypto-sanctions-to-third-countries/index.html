EU proposal widens Russia crypto sanctions to third countries

The Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions proposal would extend transaction bans to about 60 banks and target crypto networks, but needs approval from all 27 EU states.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 01:59 UTC3 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks used to help Russia evade sanctions, extending its latest sanctions package beyond Russian banks to include third countries. The proposal, presented on Nov. 23 as the EU’s 22nd package, also seeks transaction bans on about 60 Russian banks and a ban on Russian teams and athletes competing under their national flag at sporting events in the bloc.

In her statement on the 22nd package, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the crypto measures would focus on Russia and countries helping it evade existing sanctions. The Commission has also launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support that work and align efforts with international partners. Her statement did not name crypto platforms or countries, or detail the proposed restrictions on specific networks.

The third-country focus builds on a tool agreed in the EU’s 21st package, adopted in July. The Council said that measure created the possibility of a full ban on crypto-asset services in a country whose platforms help Russia evade sanctions, allowing transactions between EU operators and those providers to be prohibited. That package also extended transaction bans to 14 crypto-related service platforms based in several countries. The 22nd proposal signals a further effort to use the mechanism, but the Commission’s statement does not identify any country or say how many providers could be affected.

The distinction matters for crypto businesses operating across borders: the proposed crackdown is not described as a blanket ban on crypto activity in third countries. Rather, the Commission says it will target networks and payment channels that facilitate sanctions evasion. The practical scope will depend on how the final measures identify those channels and which providers they cover. Those details were not set out in von der Leyen’s statement.

The proposal also moves beyond crypto. The Commission wants transaction bans extended to roughly 60 Russian banks, all to be removed from the SWIFT international payments messaging system, according to von der Leyen. It proposes listing more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet and banning Russian-insured tankers from European ports. For sports, it seeks to bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at events in the EU, prohibit official Russian symbols and restrict financial flows from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural associations.

The package is not yet in force. The Associated Press reported that all 27 EU member countries must endorse the proposals before they can take effect, a process that can take weeks and may alter the measures. That leaves a gap between the Commission’s stated aim and any operational restriction on crypto services: providers and users will need the final legal text to see which transactions, platforms and jurisdictions are covered.

For comparison, the adopted 21st package already added crypto-related service platforms to transaction restrictions and established the possible countrywide mechanism. The new proposal puts enforcement against crypto and payment networks at the center of its financial measures, while pairing that with much broader bank restrictions. Whether that translates into a wider reach across third-country services, or more narrowly targets networks specifically linked to evasion, remains to be seen.

The next signals are the member states’ approval, any changes they make, and the final list of affected banks, crypto providers and payment networks. The legal text will show how the EU defines third-country assistance and what transactions EU operators must stop.
