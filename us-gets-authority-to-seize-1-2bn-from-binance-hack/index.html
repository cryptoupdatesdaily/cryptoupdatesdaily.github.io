US gets authority to seize $1.2bn from Binance hack

US courts authorized seizure or restraint of $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6 billion hack, part of more than $2.7 billion frozen since the October theft.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 14:32 UTC3 min read

US authorities have obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, adding a legal step to a recovery effort that has already frozen more than $2.7 billion. The Justice Department said on November 12 that the theft, which it attributes to North Korean military hackers known as TraderTraitor, took place on or about October 29 and exceeded $6 billion.

The distinction between frozen assets and those covered by court authorization matters: the figures describe different stages of the response, not a confirmed return of funds to Binance or its customers. In its November 12 announcement, the Justice Department said government and private-sector efforts had frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. The department had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

More than $1.2 billion has court authorization

The new authority applies to a portion of the frozen assets. The Justice Department described the action as both a seizure and restraint, but did not say that the full amount had already been transferred into government custody or forfeited. The ongoing process includes tracing, freezing, seizing and forfeiting assets, according to the department.

The scale is notable alongside the earlier freeze total: more than $1.2 billion authorized for seizure or restraint is less than half of the more than $2.7 billion frozen so far. The wider pool shows the role of private-sector coordination in the response. The department said federal agencies had worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict funds; it did not break down how much each partner froze or identify the assets’ current form.

The Associated Press reported that the FBI had described the seizure as more than $1.2 billion. Its account of the FBI announcement also put the theft at about $6.3 billion and said authorities had previously attributed it to TraderTraitor. The Justice Department’s more precise description of the legal step is court-authorized seizure or restraint.

Bridges and exchanges remain part of the chase

Recovery is still contested. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert attributing the theft and urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the Binance funds. The latest authorization strengthens the government’s ability to pursue assets it can identify and secure, while continued movement across services complicates tracing and enforcement.

The Binance theft is larger in dollar terms than the $1.5 billion Bybit exchange hack in February 2025, which the FBI also attributed to North Korea and TraderTraitor, according to the AP. That comparison puts the size of the incident in context, but does not establish how much of either theft was ultimately recovered.

The next signals are whether authorities can turn the court-authorized amount into completed seizures and forfeitures, whether the total frozen rises beyond $2.7 billion, and whether investigators can trace funds moving through bridges, mixers and exchanges. The Justice Department said efforts to recover the Binance assets are ongoing.
