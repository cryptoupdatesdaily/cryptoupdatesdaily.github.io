How to Read an Approval and Identify Its Spender

Read token approval history to identify a spender: find the address in the ERC-20 Approval event, then check it against the dapp’s official contract details.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 19:07 UTC4 min read

Open the approval transaction on the chain where it occurred and read the spender field in the token’s Approval event. That address received permission to use the token allowance, while the transaction’s From field identifies who sent the transaction and can show a different address.

An ERC-20 allowance is permission for a particular spender address to use a set amount of a particular token, for a particular wallet, on a particular chain. The Approval event records the owner, spender, and value, so the event gives you the relevant parties and allowance in one place.

Start with the wallet address and either the transaction hash or the wallet’s approval history. For background on locating the transaction itself, trace an unknown approval with revokecash; revokecash can also help you review the approvals associated with a wallet.

How do you read a token approval history?

Open the transaction on an explorer for the same chain where the approval happened. A transaction hash can be searched on that chain’s explorer; Ethereum and Base each have their own chain records, so confirm the selected network before interpreting the details.

On the transaction page, find the token contract’s Approval event or the decoded logs. Read its fields as a set: owner is the wallet granting permission, spender is the address receiving permission, and value is the allowance recorded for that token and owner.

Take a USDC approval as a simple case. The wallet appears as owner, a contract address appears as spender, and the value records the amount of USDC allowed. The spender field is the address to investigate, even when the transaction page’s From field shows a wallet or another address that submitted the transaction.

This distinction matters because a transaction sender and an approval’s spender answer different questions. From identifies the transaction sender; spender identifies the address authorized by the token owner. Looking only at From can therefore lead you to the wrong address when you are trying to identify who received token permission.

Once you have the spender address, compare it character by character with the contract address published in the dapp’s official documentation or other official contract details. Make sure the token and chain also match the approval you are investigating. An address alone is not enough to confirm that you have found the relevant contract if the chain or token differs.

The approval value adds context to the permission: it tells you how much of that token the spender was allowed to use when the event was recorded. The allowance applies to the specific token, owner, spender, and chain in that record. Treat each combination separately when reviewing more than one token or network.

How can you identify an unfamiliar contract address?

Compare the spender address from the Approval event with the protocol’s official contract details for the same chain. A match is useful evidence that the address is the published contract you were looking for; an unfamiliar or unmatched address is a reason to investigate further before deciding what to do with the approval.

For a practical walkthrough, say you find a USDC approval in your wallet history and want to know which contract received permission. Open its transaction hash on the explorer for the chain shown in the history. In the token’s decoded logs, locate Approval and note the owner, spender, and value.

Next, check that the owner matches your wallet and that the event concerns USDC on the expected chain. Copy the spender address and compare it with the dapp’s official contract address for that chain. If it matches, you have identified the published spender associated with the approval; if it does not, keep the addresses distinct and review the record before treating them as the same contract.

After identifying the spender, decide whether the allowance still fits your needs. revokecash lets you review wallet approvals and revoke one you no longer want. Recheck the token, chain, and spender before acting, since a wallet may have separate approvals for the same token on different chains.
