US moves to seize $1.2bn of crypto stolen from Binance

The Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, while over $2.7 billion is frozen.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 20:11 UTC3 min read

The US has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step toward taking legal control of assets that authorities say have already been frozen. The Justice Department announced the action on November 12, weeks after a theft the FBI attributes to North Korean hackers.

The Justice Department’s announcement on the court-authorized seizure says North Korean military members, tracked by the FBI as TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around October 29. The FBI had attributed the attack to North Korea in an October 31 alert. That attribution identifies the group officials hold responsible; the public announcement does not describe a criminal charge against named suspects.

Frozen funds exceed the amount covered by the order

More than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds has been frozen through combined US government and private-sector efforts, according to the Justice Department. The newly authorized amount—more than $1.2 billion—is a portion of that frozen total. The distinction matters: a freeze can restrict movement while ownership remains unresolved; court authorization to seize or restrain funds gives authorities a further legal route to secure them.

The FBI’s statement, reported by the Associated Press, describes the action as a seizure. The Justice Department’s announcement is more specific about the legal status: it says the department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain the funds. It does not say that all the assets have been transferred to government custody or returned to Binance customers.

The size of the frozen pool also puts the order in perspective. More than $2.7 billion frozen is less than half of the more than $6 billion the Justice Department says was stolen. The $1.2 billion covered by the authorization is less again. Authorities say they are continuing to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit assets, but have not announced a final recovery total.

Tracing the funds remains part of the work

The FBI has said TraderTraitor actors were converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and could move them through decentralised finance services and instant-swap services as part of further laundering. The Justice Department now says the actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those routes create multiple points where service providers may be able to identify or block transfers, but the government says its tracing work is ongoing.

That approach depends on coordination with virtual-asset service providers, which the FBI and its partners have urged to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering. The government’s announcement credits joint public and private efforts with freezing the larger $2.7 billion pool. It does not break down how much was frozen by US authorities, private firms or individual providers, or how much remains at addresses the attackers control.

Seizure authorization is not the same as a completed return to victims. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The announcement does not provide a timetable for that process or say how any eventual distribution would work for Binance users.

The next signals are whether authorities convert the authorization into completed seizures, how much of the remaining frozen balance proceeds to forfeiture, and whether investigators can intercept funds still moving through bridges, mixers and exchanges. For now, the confirmed change is legal authority over more than $1.2 billion within a larger pool of frozen assets—not a final accounting of what Binance or its customers will recover.
