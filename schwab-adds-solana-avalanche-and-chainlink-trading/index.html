Schwab adds Solana, Avalanche and Chainlink trading

Charles Schwab added SOL, AVAX and LINK to Schwab Crypto, lifting its direct-trading lineup to five assets while retaining a 0.75% trade fee and eligibility limits.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 10, 2026 · 12:36 UTC3 min read

Charles Schwab has added Solana, Avalanche and Chainlink to its Schwab Crypto trading service, expanding the lineup from bitcoin and ether to five digital assets. The company said the tokens became available to buy and sell on Nov. 9, after announcing plans to list them in August. In its announcement of the new listings, Schwab identified them as SOL/USD, AVAX/USD and LINK/USD.

The move brings three additional assets to a service that began rolling out to clients in May and opened to all eligible retail clients in October, according to Schwab. The company says clients can view and trade crypto alongside traditional investments on Schwab.com, Schwab Mobile and thinkorswim. That integration puts the new tokens within an existing brokerage interface; it does not make them securities or deposits, Schwab’s disclosures say.

How does the expanded lineup compare with the earlier offer?

Before the additions, Schwab Crypto offered direct trading in bitcoin and ether. The three new listings bring its total to five, a change from the August plan to make the tokens available “in the coming months” to current availability. Unchained’s report on the launch notes that Schwab did not give a specific listing date when it first announced the plan.

Schwab says the expansion is intended to offer clients more choice alongside their broader investments, and describes the selected cryptocurrencies as established assets aligned with client demand. The release did not identify what criteria it used to select these tokens or say which assets might follow. It said only that the company plans to add cryptocurrencies and other digital assets over time.

What does Schwab’s route offer, and what does it cost?

The service combines direct crypto trading with Schwab’s account interface, education materials, phone and chat support, according to the company. Schwab charges 75 basis points on the dollar value of each trade, or 0.75%. That fee is a concrete cost of using the service; Schwab describes its pricing as among the industry’s lowest, but its announcement does not provide a like-for-like comparison with other platforms.

The account’s limits matter alongside that access. Schwab Crypto is offered by Charles Schwab Premier Bank, SSB, and is available in U.S. states except New York and Louisiana; it is not available in U.S. territories or internationally, and not all clients qualify. Schwab’s disclosures say crypto in the account is not FDIC-insured, is not a bank deposit and is not protected by SIPC. The company also warns that cryptocurrency prices are volatile and investors can lose their principal.

What should clients watch after the listings?

Schwab says it may delay, modify or withdraw support for an asset because of market, regulatory, operational or risk-related developments. Those conditions make continued availability a relevant signal, as are any future listings and whether Schwab changes eligibility or pricing. The company has not named the next assets or set a timetable for further additions, so the five-token lineup is the confirmed scope for now.
