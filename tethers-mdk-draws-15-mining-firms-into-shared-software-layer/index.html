Tether’s MDK draws 15 mining firms into shared software layer

Tether says 15 mining operators, hardware makers and pools will build MDK integrations, a bid to make Bitcoin mining systems easier to automate across vendors.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 25, 2026 · 00:48 UTC3 min read

Tether says 15 Bitcoin mining operators, ASIC manufacturers and pools have committed to building and maintaining integrations for its open-source Mining Development Kit, an effort to let software work across more mining systems without a separate connection for each vendor. The company announced the commitments on October 24, saying the shared layer could support monitoring and AI-driven automation across different hardware and pools, according to Tether’s announcement of the 15 commitments.

The participants span mining operators, equipment makers and pools, including Bitdeer, BITMAIN, Canaan, Foundry Digital, F2Pool, Luxor Technology, MicroBT, OCEAN and Proto (Block, Inc.). Each is expected to build an “MDK Worker,” a translation layer that exposes selected functions of its existing hardware, pool or system through a common interface. Tether says companies retain control of their systems and choose which functions to expose.

What changes with the new commitments?

The commitments add prospective industry integrations to a kit Tether launched in April. At launch, MDK was described as an open alternative to proprietary mining software, with a JavaScript backend SDK, React UI components and a modular architecture for connecting devices and services. The new announcement shifts the emphasis from the framework itself to companies agreeing to build and maintain the adapters that could make it useful across different systems.

That matters because mining operations can combine machines from different manufacturers, separate pool connections and distinct management software. Tether says those systems often require custom integrations, making it harder to reuse applications across sites or vendors. The earlier MDK launch announcement positioned the framework against closed, proprietary tools that can limit interoperability and tie operators to particular vendors.

How does MDK compare with existing tools?

MDK’s stated approach is to connect existing systems through Workers rather than require operators to replace them or make every manufacturer and pool run the same software. Developers could then build an application against the shared interface instead of creating a separate integration for each platform. That can reduce duplicated integration work if enough participants maintain compatible Workers; it also makes coverage dependent on those participants continuing to build and support them.

Tether says its QVAC mining agent already operates directly on MDK, which it presents as an early example of an application using the common layer. The company’s April launch announcement also described Mining OS (MOS), which it had open-sourced earlier, as a system for coordinating hardware, energy and operational data. Tether said MDK could provide a development framework for MOS and other mining applications. The announcements do not establish how widely MDK or QVAC is being used beyond these examples.

What will show whether the network effect takes hold?

Tether said MDK would reach version 0.11 at the Plan ₿ Forum in Lugano on October 23–24, and set a target of a first stable 1.0 release by the end of 2026. The software is open source now, the company said, so developers can begin testing integrations before that release. The practical test is whether the announced commitments become working, maintained Workers across a meaningful share of mining equipment and pools, and whether applications can use them across vendors without repeated custom work.

For operators, the trade-off is between a shared interface that could simplify automation and the ongoing effort of integrating and maintaining their own Worker. The next signals are delivery of version 0.11, progress toward the 1.0 target, and evidence that the 15 commitments translate into functioning integrations and applications beyond Tether’s own demonstration.
