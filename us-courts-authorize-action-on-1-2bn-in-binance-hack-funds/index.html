US courts authorize action on $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US courts authorized seizure or restraint of $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, while authorities and private firms have frozen more than $2.7 billion.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 15, 2026 · 13:34 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department says courts have authorized the seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a significant legal step in a larger effort to stop the funds moving. The Justice Department’s announcement says government and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion since the theft. That does not mean all of the affected assets have been forfeited or returned to their owners.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. According to the Associated Press report, about $6.3 billion was taken from Binance in what the FBI described as the largest crypto heist in history. The Justice Department dates the theft to on or about Oct. 29 and puts the amount at more than $6 billion. The difference in those figures reflects the agencies’ and report’s wording; the announcement does not provide a final valuation.

More than $2.7 billion frozen, but recovery is not settled

The figures describe separate stages. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through joint US and private-sector efforts, while court authorization covers seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of that amount. A freeze can prevent assets from being moved; the court authorization gives authorities a legal route to seek control over a portion. Neither figure establishes that Binance or individual customers have received the cryptocurrency back.

The AP says the US government typically files a forfeiture claim in such cases, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to the property. The current announcement therefore marks progress in securing assets, but leaves ownership and distribution to later legal proceedings. It also does not say how much of the authorized amount has already been transferred into government-controlled wallets or what timetable applies to forfeiture.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

The Justice Department says investigators are still tracing, freezing, seizing and pursuing forfeiture of the stolen assets. It says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS issued an alert asking virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering, or assets derived from them.

That work depends in part on cooperation from services able to identify or restrict activity involving the funds. The announcement credits virtual asset service providers with helping interdict assets, while also describing routes through multiple services that investigators are still tracking. The case shows the practical trade-off: crypto transfers can cross platforms and services, but coordinated intervention can make some funds harder to move. The release does not specify which providers froze particular amounts or how much remains beyond their reach.

The next signals are whether authorities announce further freezes or seizures, whether the court-authorized amount proceeds to forfeiture, and whether judges identify who can claim the assets. Investigators’ continuing efforts to trace funds moving through bridges, mixers and exchanges will also indicate how much of the original theft can still be secured. For now, the $1.2 billion authorization is a legal milestone within a wider freeze effort, not confirmation of a completed return.
