Mesh and Visa link wallet balances to Visa spending

Mesh and Visa will let participating wallets route crypto and stablecoin balances into Visa purchases, removing a separate card while leaving rollout details unsettled.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 20, 2026 · 16:55 UTC3 min read

Mesh and Visa announced a partnership on Oct. 19 to let users pay in stores and online from crypto and stablecoin balances in participating wallet apps, using Visa’s merchant network without requiring a separate crypto card. The arrangement connects wallet balances to more than 175 million merchant locations across over 200 countries and territories, according to Mesh’s announcement.

Mesh says it will orchestrate the funding and conversion for each transaction. A Visa credential is provisioned behind the scenes; the customer taps at a contactless terminal or checks out online, while the merchant receives a standard payment. The companies say users will not need to sell, transfer or preload funds before paying. The announcement does not specify which assets or wallets will be supported at launch, or how conversion rates and fees will work.

How does this differ from a crypto card?

The proposed flow removes the need for a user to sign up for a card tied to one platform. Mesh says wallet and financial platforms can enable payments through its integration without building or running their own card program. It also says Mesh will act as program manager, handling onboarding and know-your-customer checks.

That changes where the payment credential sits in the user experience, but the merchant-facing transaction still runs through Visa’s network. The arrangement depends on Mesh to connect a wallet’s balance to the payment and conversion process, and on participating platforms to offer the feature. So the announcement describes a route to spend from existing wallets, not a direct crypto payment accepted by merchants.

Visa and Bridge’s previously announced model offers a useful comparison: it lets developers issue Visa cards funded by stablecoin balances, with Bridge handling backend conversion, according to The Block’s report. Mesh’s pitch is that users can spend from participating wallet apps without obtaining that separate card. Visa and WeFi also announced plans earlier this year to let users spend digital assets through Visa while keeping them in self-custody wallets, The Block reported.

When can users try the service?

There is no launch date yet. Mesh said initial availability, supported devices and participating platforms will be announced once rollout details are finalized. Its release lists Kalshi, Tools for Humanity, Félix Pago, Majority and KAST among enterprise partners, but does not say which will be among the first to enable Visa spending.

The partnership therefore sets out infrastructure and intended functionality, rather than a live service users can access now. The scale of Visa’s network gives the proposal broad potential reach, but actual access will depend on platform participation and the eventual rollout. The announcement also leaves open the consumer-facing terms: supported assets, conversion timing, exchange rates and fees.

What will show whether the model works?

The next signals are the first participating wallets, launch markets and devices, followed by details on supported balances and transaction costs. Those will show how much control users retain over which asset funds a purchase, and what conversion costs them. The companies have not yet published those details or transaction volume targets.

For now, the key distinction is between a wallet-native checkout experience and a conventional crypto card: Mesh says it can remove the separate card step, while Visa continues to provide the merchant acceptance network. Whether that is meaningfully simpler for users will depend on where the feature launches and how clearly the final payment terms are disclosed.
