Mastercard opens AI agent payment tools to developers

Mastercard has opened Agent Pay for Machines to developers after pilots, pairing x402 payment requests with spending limits and settlement through crypto and fiat rails.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 10:28 UTC3 min read

Mastercard opened early access to Agent Pay for Machines on Oct. 20, giving developers APIs, a toolkit and a sandbox to test payments by AI agents. The system builds on x402, an open protocol for online payment requests, and is designed to let agents buy digital services within budgets set by businesses, according to Mastercard’s announcement. The move follows end-to-end pilots with Cloudflare, Ant International, AIsa, Base, BVNK, Coinbase and Hype.

The announcement moves the project from pilots to developer testing, not a general commercial launch. Mastercard first announced Agent Pay for Machines in June; it now says the first buyer- and seller-side APIs are available through early access. The Block reported that the program remains in pilot stage and is not yet commercially available.

How does an agent make a payment without an account at every service?

Under the flow Mastercard describes, a business gives an agent a task and spending limit. When a paid service responds with “402 Payment Required,” the agent creates a digitally signed voucher promising payment within that limit. The provider verifies the voucher and supplies the service; vouchers can be combined and redeemed later. Mastercard says these interactions happen off-chain at machine speed.

The approach targets a friction point in the existing model: paid digital services often require a separate account, API key, prepaid credit balance or billing relationship. Those arrangements can work when an agent uses only a few providers, but become harder when it selects services as a task unfolds. With x402, payment is requested at the point of use; Mastercard adds a layer connecting the flow to its network. The trade-off is that the signed voucher represents a promise to pay, with redemption later, rather than each service being settled on-chain immediately.

What did the pilot show about the crypto and fiat rails?

At Cloudflare Connect, marketing agency Hype gave an agent a budget to plan a sample protein-bar campaign. It bought social-media research from AIsa and eight campaign images through Cloudflare AI Gateway: nine payments to two merchants under one approved budget, according to Mastercard. The company said the demonstration needed no separate accounts, API keys or invoices.

The crypto infrastructure handled specific parts of the flow. Coinbase Developer Platform provided wallet infrastructure to fund the agent, while Base was the on-chain settlement network. BVNK acted as the agent wallet and used Mastercard’s Global Money Platform to process payment and settlement in fiat or stablecoins; sellers can receive funds in their preferred currency, token or rail. The Block’s report likewise describes Coinbase and Base’s roles and says Mastercard is seeking developer feedback. The setup therefore combines an open payment-request protocol and blockchain settlement with a payment network and multiple settlement options.

What will show whether the system can move beyond a demo?

Early access gives developers a chance to test the buyer- and seller-side tools, but the announced demonstration was a controlled task with an approved budget. Mastercard says it expects the roadmap to be shaped by customer and partner needs. The next signals are whether developers use the sandbox for more services and payment flows, what changes follow their feedback, and when Mastercard makes the system commercially available. The announcement does not set a date for that wider launch.
