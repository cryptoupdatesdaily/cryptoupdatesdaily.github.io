US wins authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

A US court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen as tracing efforts continue.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 15, 2026 · 21:39 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step that could secure part of the proceeds while investigators pursue the rest. The Justice Department said on November 12 that the authorized amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through combined government and private-sector efforts. Those figures mark progress against a theft the FBI attributes to North Korean actors, but they do not mean the funds have been forfeited or returned.

The Justice Department’s announcement of the court-authorized seizure and restraint says the theft took place on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The department identifies the actors as members of the North Korean military, known to the FBI as TraderTraitor. The Associated Press report on the FBI’s seizure announcement describes the theft as about $6.3 billion and says the bureau had previously attributed it to the same group.

Freezes cover more than the court-authorized amount

The gap between the two recovery figures matters. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen, while the Justice Department says courts have authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of that total. A freeze can stop assets from moving while authorities pursue legal control; the announcement does not say that all frozen assets have been transferred to government custody. Nor does it establish that the full authorized amount has already been forfeited.

That leaves a substantial portion of the theft outside the amount currently covered by the stated seizure authority. The $1.2 billion is less than one-fifth of the approximately $6.3 billion reported stolen, although the broader frozen total is larger. These comparisons describe the scale of the response, not a final recovery rate: the Justice Department says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture work is continuing.

Tracing and forfeiture remain open

The FBI’s October 31 alert said investigators had observed TraderTraitor converting stolen assets primarily into Ether and expected further laundering through decentralized finance and instant-swap services. It urged virtual-asset providers and related services to block transactions involving listed addresses or funds derived from them. The Justice Department now says the actors continue to move assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, underscoring why investigators are coordinating with service providers as they try to interrupt transfers.

Seizure authority is also distinct from deciding who ultimately receives the property. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The current announcement therefore establishes a legal route to secure assets, not a final decision on ownership or compensation.

The Justice Department also announced action against a separate North Korean-linked scheme, FaceStealer, which it says stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. That figure concerns a different operation and should not be added to the Binance theft or its recovery totals. The next signals are whether authorities obtain further seizure orders, whether frozen assets withstand forfeiture proceedings, and how much of the stolen crypto can still be traced as laundering continues.
