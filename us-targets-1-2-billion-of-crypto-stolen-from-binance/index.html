US targets $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, within $2.7 billion frozen so far, as tracing continues.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 13, 2026 · 13:48 UTC3 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, a step that puts a legal process behind part of the much larger effort to freeze the stolen assets. The department said on Thursday that the amount is included in more than $2.7 billion frozen so far by US agencies and private-sector partners, according to its announcement on actions against North Korean crypto theft.

Court authority covers less than half the frozen total

The distinction between frozen funds and assets covered by court authorization matters. A freeze can prevent funds from moving while investigators trace them; authorization to seize or restrain gives authorities legal grounds to pursue control of the specified assets. The Justice Department did not say that the full $1.2 billion has already been transferred into government custody or forfeited. The Associated Press reported that prosecutors typically file a forfeiture claim, after which a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The figures also show the scale of the gap between locating assets and recovering them through legal process. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through combined government and private-sector work, while court authorization currently covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount. The figures do not establish how much will ultimately be returned to Binance or other claimants, or how much remains beyond authorities’ reach.

The $6 billion theft is still being traced

The department says the theft took place on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The FBI attributes it to North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor. That attribution is the US government’s assessment; the current announcement concerns the recovery effort and does not disclose technical evidence behind the attribution.

Authorities say the work is ongoing because the actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI, working with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets. That approach depends in part on cooperation from services that can identify or stop transactions passing through their platforms, while funds moving through other routes remain harder to intercept.

Freezing is not the same as returning funds

The enforcement effort is separate from Binance’s own response to the theft. The Justice Department’s figures describe assets frozen or covered by court authorization; they do not say whether Binance has replaced customer balances or how the company is accounting for losses. Nor does the announcement provide a final accounting of the hack. The AP’s report on the seizure says the theft was estimated at about $6.3 billion, while the Justice Department describes more than $6 billion, a difference in the reported totals.

The immediate signals to watch are whether the court-authorized assets move into completed seizures, whether prosecutors file forfeiture claims and how much of the frozen total can be traced and legally recovered. The government says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts continue; it has not given a timeline for their completion.
