DeFi lending vault vs money market fund: Keep emergency cash safer

DeFi lending vault vs money market fund for cash yield: a fund suits most spare cash; use a stablecoin vault only for a small, risk-ready slice.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 01:03 UTC4 min read

For most spare cash, a money market fund is the better default; a DeFi lending vault is worth considering only for a small slice you already hold as stablecoins and can afford to lose in full.

How to choose between a vault and a fund

A money market fund invests in short-term debt, so its yield generally follows short-term policy rates. US money market funds operate under SEC Rule 2a-7, which sets limits intended to manage risks such as credit and liquidity; the fund is still an investment, not an insured bank deposit. A stablecoin vault earns from borrowers paying to borrow crypto, so its yield can rise when demand is strong and fall below a fund’s yield when demand weakens.

Morpho vaults are one example: they pool a single asset such as USDC and allocate it across lending markets and other supported yield sources. That feature makes them relevant to this comparison: the return depends on crypto lending activity, rather than the short-term debt markets that drive a money market fund. Aave is another familiar DeFi lending name, but the risk here depends on the specific vault and the markets it uses.

Compare the yield gap only after counting the risks and costs that come with moving cash on-chain. A higher displayed rate is compensation for smart-contract, oracle, collateral and stablecoin depeg risks. A vault may also face losses if borrowers fail to repay and collateral does not cover the debt. Stablecoin deposits in DeFi vaults have no FDIC or SIPC protection.

  • Yield source: Compare the expected vault yield with the fund’s current yield, then ask what could move each one. Fund yields generally track short-term rates; vault yields follow borrower demand and can drop quickly when fewer users want to borrow.
  • Principal protections: Identify what stands behind the balance. Rule 2a-7 places requirements on US money market funds, but does not make them risk-free or FDIC-insured. A stablecoin vault has no equivalent government backstop, and the stablecoin itself can lose its peg.
  • Liquidity: Check when you can actually access the money. Fund redemptions follow the fund’s settlement rules. Vault withdrawals depend on available unborrowed liquidity in its markets; if most assets are lent out, a withdrawal may be delayed until borrowers repay or liquidity returns.
  • Round-trip costs: Add the exchange spread and network fee to move fiat into stablecoins, plus the cost to convert back and withdraw. Take an illustrative short holding: if those costs consume the expected yield gap for the whole period, the higher vault rate has not improved your result. Estimate the gap over your intended holding period and subtract both entry and exit costs before deciding.
  • Holding period and purpose: Keep emergency money in the fund, where access follows its stated redemption timetable. Consider a vault only for a small portion you do not need soon and could lose entirely. The ceiling is the amount you can genuinely afford to lose, not the amount a projected yield makes tempting.

Before moving fiat, write down the expected yield difference for your holding period and the full round-trip costs. Then ask whether the remaining gain is worth the additional risks and slower or uncertain access. If you already hold stablecoins on-chain for the long term, the conversion costs disappear; that makes a vault more competitive, but it does not remove lending, contract or depeg risk.

Related questions

Are stablecoins safe for holding savings?

Stablecoins are not the same as insured cash, and their value can move away from the currency they are designed to track. In a vault, you take both the stablecoin risk and the risks of the lending markets and contracts holding or deploying it. Do not treat a dollar target as a guarantee of getting one dollar back.

Can you lose money in a money market fund?

Yes. A money market fund can lose value, even though many seek to maintain a stable share price. The fund’s rules and short-term debt holdings make its risk profile different from a DeFi vault’s; they do not guarantee principal. Check the fund’s type, redemption terms and stated risks before using it for cash.

Is a stablecoin vault’s yield taxed like interest?

Tax treatment depends on your country and how the activity is structured. Do not assume vault rewards receive the same treatment as fund distributions or bank interest; keep transaction and reward records, and check the rules that apply where you file.

The answer can change if your cash needs change or the yield gap moves after costs. Revisit the choice when you need the money sooner, when a vault’s borrower demand weakens, or when you already hold stablecoins and can leave them invested longer; emergency cash still belongs in the fund.
