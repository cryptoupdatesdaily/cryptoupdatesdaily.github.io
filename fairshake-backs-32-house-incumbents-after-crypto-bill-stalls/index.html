Fairshake backs 32 House incumbents after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake named 32 House incumbents for midterm support, disclosing $6 million for six as the Senate stall leaves crypto policy’s next move to elections.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 8, 2026 · 21:44 UTC3 min read

Fairshake, the crypto industry’s major political group, said on Oct. 5 it would support 32 House incumbents in November’s midterms, putting election spending behind lawmakers who backed a market-structure bill now stalled in the Senate. The slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats; six will receive $1 million each, according to The Associated Press’s report on Fairshake’s House slate.

The announcement shifts attention from legislation to the candidates who supported it. The House passed the CLARITY Act in July 2025, but the Senate blocked a procedural vote in September 2026 after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics provisions addressing public officials’ crypto interests, according to AP’s account of the Senate vote. Fairshake’s support gives the industry a way to back those lawmakers while the bill’s path forward remains uncertain.

Who gets the disclosed $1 million commitments?

Three Democrats—Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California—and three Republicans—French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan—are set for $1 million each. Roll Call reported that Fairshake did not disclose planned spending on the other 26 candidates.

The wider slate includes prominent lawmakers such as House Majority Whip Tom Emmer, Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar and Ways and Means Chairman Jason Smith. The bipartisan spread follows the candidates’ votes on crypto policy, rather than a single party alignment: Roll Call reported that all 32 backed the CLARITY Act. That gives Fairshake relationships across both parties, but support for one bill does not establish that every backed candidate agrees with the industry on every crypto issue.

How does the House slate compare with Fairshake’s other spending?

The six disclosed House commitments total $6 million, a smaller announced sum than Fairshake’s planned spending against Sherrod Brown, the Democratic Senate candidate in Ohio. AP reported that Fairshake pledged nearly $30 million against Brown, who is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted. Brown also faced nearly $40 million in crypto spending in 2024, when he lost to Bernie Moreno.

The contrast shows that the House announcement is only a partial view of the group’s election plans. AP reported that Fairshake and its affiliated PACs held about $120 million in cash at the end of August. The group did not say whether it would spend more in House or Senate races before November, and it did not disclose allocations for the other 26 House candidates. The $6 million therefore describes only the six named commitments, not the full value of the 32-candidate slate.

What could the spending change while the bill is stalled?

The CLARITY Act aimed to establish a federal regulatory framework for digital assets and divide oversight among federal regulators. Its supporters argued that clearer rules would help the industry; opponents focused on ethics safeguards and President Donald Trump’s crypto interests. The failed Senate vote leaves those disputes unresolved as the election approaches.

Backing incumbents who supported the bill may help Fairshake preserve congressional support for a future attempt, but the announcement alone does not determine whether a new bill will advance. The next signals are the group’s spending disclosures for the remaining House races, any further Senate investments, and whether negotiations over ethics provisions resume after the midterms.
