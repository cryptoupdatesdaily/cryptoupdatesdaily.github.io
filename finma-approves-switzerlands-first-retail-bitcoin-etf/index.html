FINMA approves Switzerland’s first retail Bitcoin ETF

FINMA approved Switzerland’s first retail fund investing directly in Bitcoin, adding a regulated fund route while leaving investors exposed to sharp price swings.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 09:17 UTC3 min read

Switzerland’s financial regulator FINMA approved the country’s first exchange-traded retail fund investing directly in Bitcoin on 20 October, opening a new regulated route to Bitcoin exposure while warning that investors can still lose a significant part of their capital. FINMA classifies it as an “other fund for alternative investments” with a specific risk under Swiss law. Its approval announcement says other providers have also submitted or announced applications.

BIT Asset Management says FINMA approved its Bitcoin Fund ETF, ticker BBIT, and that it expects the fund to begin trading on SIX Swiss Exchange in November. The approval is a change in access and fund structure, not a claim that Bitcoin’s price risk has changed. FINMA says the asset’s volatility remains high even inside an approved fund.

What does FINMA’s approval change for Swiss investors?

It permits a publicly available Swiss fund to invest directly in Bitcoin. The previous Swiss milestone was narrower: FINMA authorised a fund in 2021 that invested mainly in a basket of cryptoassets and was limited to qualified investors. The new approval therefore extends the fund route to retail investors and focuses on Bitcoin alone, according to the regulator.

BIT Asset Management describes BBIT as a spot fund that holds Bitcoin directly, rather than futures or other derivatives. The company says its assets will be held separately from the management company and ring-fenced for investors. Those structural features distinguish the proposed fund from many European Bitcoin products, which BIT says are exchange-traded debt instruments carrying issuer risk. That comparison comes from the manager; the regulator’s announcement does not assess those other products.

Investors would still bear Bitcoin’s market movements. A fund wrapper can provide regulated governance and access through an ordinary brokerage account, but it does not remove the possibility of a large loss if Bitcoin falls. FINMA says fund documents must explain risks clearly, while supervised institutions must address operational and custody risks under its January 2026 guidance.

Why did the regulator allow a Bitcoin-only fund?

FINMA approved an exemption from the applicable risk diversification rules. It said Bitcoin’s longer trading history and particularly high trading volume compared with other cryptocurrencies supported that decision. This addresses the fund’s concentration in one asset; it does not amount to a finding that Bitcoin is low risk. FINMA explicitly warns that the investment remains highly risky and says firms must give clear warnings about volatility and potential losses.

The approval also differs from direct ownership of Bitcoin. A fund brings Swiss fund rules and point-of-sale conduct requirements into the investment process, which FINMA says can provide greater investor protection than buying cryptocurrency directly. That protection concerns the framework around distribution, custody and disclosure, rather than a guarantee of value or capital.

What should investors watch before the fund trades?

The next near-term signal is whether BBIT begins trading on SIX as BIT Asset Management expects in November. FINMA says other fund providers have applications submitted or planned, so further approvals could show whether the first decision becomes a broader Swiss retail-fund category. The regulator’s announcement does not name those applicants or give a timetable.

Investors can also compare the final fund documents with the regulator’s stated requirements: how custody and operational risks are handled, how Bitcoin concentration is disclosed, and what warnings appear at the point of sale. Until trading begins, approval establishes the product’s regulatory status, while its market performance, access and investor uptake remain to be seen.
