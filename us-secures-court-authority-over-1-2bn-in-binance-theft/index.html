US secures court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department says court orders cover more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack proceeds, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far as recovery and forfeiture continue.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 14, 2026 · 15:53 UTC3 min read

U.S. authorities have obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The figure is part of a larger effort: government agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department’s announcement.

The FBI says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. It attributed the operation to members of the North Korean military known as TraderTraitor. The $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is therefore only part of the reported loss; the broader freeze total shows why the distinction between locating or immobilizing funds and securing legal authority over them matters.

Frozen assets and court orders are different measures

The Justice Department describes the $2.7 billion as frozen through collective U.S. government and private-sector action. It says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that amount. Those figures should not be read as two separate recoveries: the court-authorized amount sits within the larger frozen total.

A freeze can block funds from moving while authorities investigate and pursue legal action. Court authorization advances that process, but it does not by itself establish who is entitled to the assets or mean that victims have received repayment. The Associated Press reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

That leaves a gap between the headline figure and the final outcome. The orders give authorities legal grounds to pursue a substantial share of the identified proceeds, while the wider frozen pool remains subject to tracing and further proceedings. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

According to the Justice Department, TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. That makes the pace and extent of further recovery dependent on continued tracing and cooperation with virtual asset service providers. The department says law enforcement partners have worked with those providers to interdict the funds.

Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert attributing the theft to TraderTraitor and urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving, or derived from, addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets. That warning is one part of the response; the new court-authorized amount marks a legal step alongside the operational work of freezing funds.

The Justice Department’s announcement also described a separate North Korean-linked operation, FaceStealer, which officials say stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. The department presented the Binance action and FaceStealer disruption together as part of a broader response to North Korean crypto theft, but the schemes are distinct. The FaceStealer figure is not included in the Binance recovery totals.

For now, the key measures to watch are whether authorities and service providers freeze more of the remaining Binance-linked assets, how much of the frozen pool receives court authorization for seizure or restraint, and what forfeiture proceedings establish about claims to recovered funds. The government says tracing and recovery work is continuing; it has not announced a final distribution of the assets.
