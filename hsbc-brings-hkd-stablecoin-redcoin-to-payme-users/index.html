HSBC brings HKD stablecoin RedCoin to PayMe users

HSBC has launched its HKD stablecoin through PayMe, giving more than 3.3 million users a new payment option while setting up a test of retail demand and trust.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 25, 2026 · 02:52 UTC4 min read

HSBC launched its Hong Kong dollar stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), on PayMe on 23 October, extending the bank’s digital-asset services into everyday payments for a potential audience of more than 3.3 million users. The bank says users will be able to activate an in-app wallet in the coming days and send RedCoin to other people or pay merchants. HSBC’s launch announcement says the coin is licensed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); the South China Morning Post’s report describes a phased rollout.

The launch moves HSBC’s stablecoin from a plan announced earlier this year to a consumer payment product. Its first uses are deliberately narrow: person-to-person transfers and payments at merchants through PayMe. HSBC says it plans to add the wallet to its Hong Kong mobile banking app later this year, alongside access to tokenised investment products. That leaves the broader investment and institutional ambitions for later, while the initial test is whether existing PayMe users will adopt a new form of Hong Kong dollar payment.

What can PayMe users do with RedCoin now?

Users will be able to activate a stablecoin wallet inside PayMe and use it for transfers and merchant payments, according to HSBC. The bank says payments will be available at more than 100,000 merchants through PayMe. The SCMP reported that PayMe head Lawrence Li said users would not need a minimum balance or an additional account-opening process, and that transfers would use an interface similar to the existing app.

The app distribution is a practical advantage over asking customers to find and set up a separate crypto wallet. It also keeps RedCoin’s initial reach tied to PayMe’s existing payment network: the launch announcement does not describe open transfers to other stablecoin networks or wallets. HSBC’s product information says the PayMe and future HSBC HK app wallets will be separate and RedCoin will not be transferable between them.

How does RedCoin differ from HSBC’s other digital assets?

RedCoin is designed for retail payments, whereas HSBC says its existing Hong Kong digital-asset services include digital bonds for institutional investors, a retail gold token and a tokenised deposit service for wholesale payments. Those products serve different users and purposes; RedCoin adds an HKD-referenced coin for PayMe transactions rather than replacing them.

HSBC says RedCoin is fully backed by segregated, liquid assets and redeemable one-to-one for Hong Kong dollars, subject to its terms. It is issued under the bank’s HKMA stablecoin licence, granted in April 2026. But the bank also states that RedCoin is not a protected deposit under Hong Kong’s Deposit Protection Scheme. The stablecoin therefore offers a regulated payment format with bank infrastructure, while remaining distinct from a bank deposit in that specific protection.

What will show whether the rollout is gaining traction?

The first signal will be whether activation spreads beyond the initial rollout and whether users use RedCoin for both transfers and purchases at PayMe merchants. HSBC says staff pilots involving nearly 100 people tested the process from minting to live transfers, but public use will test the service at a larger scale.

Transparency will also matter as circulation grows. HSBC’s product page says it will publish circulation and reserve information and regular independent attestations, with the first report expected after the fourth quarter of 2026. The bank also plans to bring RedCoin to its mobile banking app and let customers subscribe to tokenised investments, including a planned share class of its Hong Kong dollar money market fund. Those additions would broaden the use case; until then, merchant activity, reserve reporting and the pace of wallet rollout are the clearest measures of whether the launch has moved beyond distribution into routine use.
