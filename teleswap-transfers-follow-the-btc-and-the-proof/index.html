TeleSwap transfers: follow the BTC and the proof

TeleSwap lockers vs relayers roles: relayers prove Bitcoin deposits on-chain; Lockers hold BTC, back it with collateral and pay it out when teleBTC is burned.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 06:35 UTC4 min read

TeleSwap Lockers hold the Bitcoin behind a wrap, while relayers carry Bitcoin block headers to the EVM chain so its contracts can verify transactions. The roles are separate because verification happens on-chain, while custody and payout sit with a collateralized Locker. When tracing a 0.1 BTC route, use TeleSwap to identify the service, then follow the relay proof and Locker payout as distinct events.

TeleSwap Lockers vs relayers: follow a 0.1 BTC transfer

On a wrap, you send 0.1 BTC to a Locker’s Bitcoin address with the request data needed to associate that deposit with a recipient. The Locker is the party holding the BTC. It also locks collateral, which can be slashed if it fails to meet its payout obligation.

A relayer handles the evidence. It submits Bitcoin block headers to the relay contract on Polygon. Those headers let the contract check the Bitcoin chain and verify that the deposit transaction is included in a block. The transaction’s Merkle inclusion proof connects the specific deposit to that verified block.

That step matters because the EVM contract needs a way to confirm an event that happened on Bitcoin before it can authorize the corresponding token action. The relayer supplies data for the check; it does not receive or hold the user’s BTC or teleBTC. TeleportDAO’s Bitcoin-to-EVM bridge contract repository describes its light-client approach to making Bitcoin data available on EVM chains.

Once the proof passes verification and the Locker’s custody is recorded, the contract can mint teleBTC to the named recipient on Polygon. In this example, the 0.1 BTC stays with the Locker, while the recipient gets the corresponding wrapped asset after the deposit is proven. The mint depends on valid Bitcoin data reaching the contract and passing its checks, not on the relayer taking possession of the deposit.

That distinction separates two kinds of delay and risk. If relayers stop submitting headers, the contract may lack current data to verify a deposit, so minting can wait. A relayer delay affects the route’s progress; it does not make the relayer a custodian of the BTC. A Locker failure is different: the Locker is responsible for funds it holds, and collateral can be slashed if it does not pay out as required.

On unwrap, the direction reverses. The user burns teleBTC through the contract and requests BTC to a Bitcoin address. The Locker sends the BTC, and the payment is then proven through the relay process. The proof lets the contract verify the payout event on Bitcoin. So the same division persists both ways: relayers carry data used for verification, while the Locker performs the BTC custody and payment.

For a transfer review, write down three things separately: the Bitcoin deposit or payout, the header and inclusion proof that establish it, and the Locker responsible for holding or sending the BTC. The TeleSwap SDK’s request fields expose transaction details such as a Locker and Bitcoin address, which can help you connect the request to the relevant on-chain and Bitcoin events.

Related questions

How do you become a TeleSwap Locker?

Becoming a Locker means taking on a custody and payout role, with collateral at risk if the required BTC is not paid. TeleportDAO’s founder described Lockers as decentralized Bitcoin custodians in a KuCoin interview about TeleSwap roles. Check the project’s current requirements before treating participation as open or assuming a particular application process.

Who runs TeleSwap relayers?

Relayers are the operators that submit Bitcoin block headers to the EVM relay contract. They provide chain data for verification; they do not custody a user’s BTC or teleBTC.

Locker and relayer FAQ

What collateral do TeleSwap Lockers use?

TeleportDAO’s founder identified TST as collateral for TeleSwap Lockers. The collateral supports the Locker’s payout obligation and can be slashed for failure to pay.

What happens if a Locker goes offline?

A Locker that cannot complete a required payout creates a custody and redemption problem. The protocol’s slashing mechanism can penalize a Locker that fails to pay, while a relayer outage instead delays the data needed for verification.

Can anyone run a TeleSwap relayer?

The role is to submit Bitcoin headers for contract verification. The sources cited here do not establish that anyone can join permissionlessly, so check current project requirements before assuming open participation.

How is a TeleSwap Locker chosen for my deposit?

The deposit request identifies a Locker’s Bitcoin address. Follow that request’s address and associated proof to see which Locker is responsible for custody; do not infer the Locker from the relayer that submits headers.

For a 0.1 BTC transfer, the key decision is whether you are checking proof progress or BTC custody: the relayer answers the first question, and the Locker answers the second.
