DOJ wins authority over $1.2 billion in Binance theft

The Justice Department won authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from Binance’s $6.3 billion theft, while recovery and forfeiture remain ongoing.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 18, 2026 · 19:43 UTC3 min read

The Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, putting a legal process behind part of a much larger effort to freeze the funds. The department said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and attributed it to North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor, according to its Nov. 12 announcement.

The distinction between freezing and seizing matters. U.S. agencies and private-sector partners have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, the department said, while court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that total. The authorized amount is therefore a subset of the frozen funds, not a separate recovery on top of them. The department’s wording also covers assets it may “seize or restrain”; it does not say that the full amount has been forfeited or returned to victims.

The court order covers part of the reported $6.3 billion theft

The FBI describes the Binance theft as more than $6 billion; the Associated Press reported the amount as about $6.3 billion and called it the largest crypto heist in history. AP also reported that a judge typically determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds after the government files a forfeiture claim. That leaves a gap between stopping assets from moving and resolving who receives them, as AP’s report on the seizure explains.

The scale of the freeze gives the operation a different near-term measure of progress from the headline court-authorized figure. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through combined government and private-sector work, according to the Justice Department. Its announcement says law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the funds. That coordination has helped keep a portion of the stolen assets within reach, but the release does not break down how much was frozen by each partner or how much has already been converted into a final forfeiture.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

Authorities say the work is still underway because TraderTraitor actors continue to launder the assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those routes make the recovery effort a moving target: officials must trace the funds and work with services that may be able to block transactions. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation previously urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the actors use to launder the Binance funds, the department said.

The FBI had attributed the theft to TraderTraitor before the seizure announcement. The new development is the court authorization and the reported combined freeze, not a fresh finding about who carried out the attack. The Justice Department’s announcement also covered a separate North Korean-linked operation called FaceStealer, but it described that as a distinct social-engineering campaign and not part of the Binance theft.

The next signals are whether the government files forfeiture claims, how much of the frozen balance it ultimately seeks to seize, and whether investigators can keep tracing funds as they move across services. Until those steps are reported, the $1.2 billion figure marks court-authorized action within the recovery effort, rather than a confirmed return of that amount to Binance or its customers.
