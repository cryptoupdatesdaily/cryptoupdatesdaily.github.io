US secures authority over $1.2 billion in Binance theft

The Justice Department says a court authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion, part of $2.7 billion frozen after Binance’s October theft.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 14, 2026 · 15:28 UTC3 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as authorities and private-sector partners continue efforts to trace the assets. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion frozen since the theft, according to the Justice Department’s November 12 announcement.

The distinction between frozen funds and assets covered by the court authorization matters: the $1.2 billion is not described as money already returned to Binance or its customers. The department says it has obtained authorization to seize or restrain that portion of the larger frozen total. It does not say in the announcement how much has been finally forfeited or distributed.

More than $2.7 billion frozen from a theft above $6 billion

The department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. Against that reported theft, the $2.7 billion frozen so far represents less than half; the $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is a smaller portion still. Those comparisons describe the scale of the response, not the amount recovered for victims.

The freeze total combines U.S. government and private-sector action. The Justice Department says its teams worked with virtual asset service providers to interdict the stolen assets. That cooperation gives authorities a way to act when assets reach services able to block transactions, while the overall figure also shows that the reported theft remains substantially larger than the funds frozen to date.

The Record, a cybersecurity publication from Recorded Future News, likewise reported that the court-authorized amount was part of the $2.7 billion frozen by crypto platforms and other partners. Its account, like the department’s, describes an ongoing effort rather than a completed recovery.

Tracing continues as funds move through crypto services

The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those routes can complicate efforts to follow and stop transfers across services. Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert urging virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses the group uses to launder the Binance assets, including transactions derived from those addresses.

The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. That list points to distinct stages: tracing and freezing can limit movement, while seizure and forfeiture address government control and legal claims to the property. The announcement does not specify the court proceedings behind the authorization or set out when the funds could be returned.

For Binance users and the wider market, the immediate measure is therefore the gap between the reported loss and the amount frozen, alongside how much of the frozen total proceeds through seizure and forfeiture. The next signals to watch are updates from U.S. authorities on additional funds blocked, any public forfeiture actions, and whether the government clarifies how claims to the assets will be resolved.
