Fairshake commits $6 million across six House races

Fairshake is backing 32 House incumbents, six with $1 million each, as Senate gridlock over the CLARITY Act shifts crypto’s campaign push toward November races.

By Blockchain Today EditorialOct 6, 2026 · 17:09 UTC3 min read

Fairshake, a major crypto political spending group, has named 32 House candidates it plans to support in November’s midterm elections, including six slated to receive $1 million each. The bipartisan slate comes after the Senate blocked the industry-backed CLARITY Act in September, shifting attention from a stalled federal rulemaking effort to which lawmakers will sit in the next Congress, according to The Associated Press’s report on Fairshake’s slate.

Who is on Fairshake’s list, and how much is disclosed?

The list has 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats; Fairshake has named $1 million in support for three members of each party. The six are Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan, and Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, according to Roll Call’s account of the spending plan.

Hill chairs the House Financial Services Committee, Steil chairs its digital assets subcommittee, and Huizenga is the panel’s vice chair. The broader slate includes senior party leaders and committee chairs, among them Democratic Caucus Chair Pete Aguilar, Republican Majority Whip Tom Emmer and Ways and Means Chairman Jason Smith. Fairshake has not disclosed the amounts it intends to spend on the other 26 candidates, Roll Call reported.

The group and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash on hand at the end of August, the AP reported. That figure indicates capacity, not a commitment to spend the full balance in House races. Fairshake declined to say whether it would make further House or Senate investments before November.

How did the stalled bill change the political backdrop?

The House passed the CLARITY Act in July 2025. It would establish rules for digital asset markets and divide oversight between federal regulators, but the Senate did not reach agreement on the bill’s provisions. Democrats raised concerns that its ethics safeguards did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto interests; the Senate blocked the legislation in September, according to the AP and Roll Call.

Fairshake says it backs pro-crypto candidates in both parties. Roll Call reported that all 32 candidates on the new list supported the bill. The vote record gives the slate a policy connection, though it does not show whether campaign spending itself will change lawmakers’ positions or secure passage in the next Congress.

The House slate also sits alongside a much larger announced Senate effort: Fairshake said in September it would spend nearly $30 million opposing Ohio Democrat Sherrod Brown, who is challenging Republican Sen. Jon Husted. The AP reported that crypto groups spent nearly $40 million against Brown in 2024, when he lost to Republican Bernie Moreno. Against that precedent, the six disclosed House allocations are substantial for individual races, but smaller than the group’s announced Ohio campaign.

What should readers watch before November?

First, watch for Fairshake’s actual spending and any additional candidates or races it targets: the 32-name slate identifies recipients of support, but the amounts for most remain unknown. Second, the Senate campaign against Brown will show whether the group continues to focus its largest general-election spending on lawmakers it views as obstacles to crypto legislation. Finally, the November results will indicate whether backing candidates across party lines translates into a larger group of lawmakers willing to advance market-structure rules. Fairshake says it wants to build the largest pro-crypto caucus in Congress; whether that caucus can bridge the unresolved ethics dispute is still open.
