US Seeks Forfeiture of $1.1B in Binance Hack USDC

A civil filing seeks more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to the Binance theft, expanding a recovery effort while leaving final forfeiture to the courts.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 12:55 UTC3 min read

The United States filed a civil forfeiture action on Nov. 23 seeking more than $1.1 billion in USDC tied to the October theft from Binance, moving the recovery effort from asset restraint toward a court decision on forfeiture. The Justice Department’s announcement says the assets are proceeds of the theft or funds laundered from it; the filing does not mean the government has already taken final ownership.

The complaint names USDC, the stablecoin issued by Circle Internet Financial, and identifies cryptocurrency addresses holding the assets on multiple networks. It says the funds had already been subject to a government-requested restraining order. The civil forfeiture complaint, filed in the District of Columbia, asks the court to forfeit that property to the United States.

The amount is a portion of a much larger loss. The complaint says unauthorized actors accessed Binance’s network and hot wallets on or about Oct. 29, 2026, and obtained approximately $6 billion in virtual currency. U.S. agencies assess that the theft was carried out by North Korean military hacking groups known as Lazarus Group and APT38, based on technical indicators and laundering patterns, according to the filing.

The complaint describes a route into Binance’s systems that began with a purported job interview. Binance told law enforcement that an employee was likely compromised after a recruiter prompted them to download software containing malware. The filing says the attackers then used an EIP-7702 injection to gain control of hot wallets and deploy a “sweeper” contract that moved funds as they arrived. These are allegations and investigative findings presented by the government, not a court ruling on responsibility.

That account gives the forfeiture action a different role from the earlier restraints. Freezing funds can limit their movement while investigators trace them; the new case asks a court to determine whether the specified assets can be forfeited. The filing concerns USDC identified at listed addresses, rather than the full range of assets taken in the hack. It therefore advances recovery of one traced portion, while the broader effort depends on locating and disrupting other funds as they move across wallets and services.

The Justice Department said the government had assisted in freezing, seizing or returning more than $2.9 billion in cryptocurrency from multiple sources since the Binance theft. Its Nov. 12 announcement had reported more than $2.7 billion frozen through government and private-sector efforts, with court authorization to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion of that amount. Those figures describe different stages and scopes of recovery; they should not be read as the amount returned to Binance or as additional sums on top of the current claim.

The next signals are the court’s handling of the forfeiture complaint, whether the named USDC remains restrained through the case, and any further filings identifying assets or tracing activity. The FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation are investigating, and the Justice Department says work to trace and recover assets continues. The outcome will show how much of the identified stablecoin can be legally recovered; it will not, by itself, settle the status of the remaining reported loss.
