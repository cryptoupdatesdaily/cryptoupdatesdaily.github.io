What does total value locked mean in DeFi lending? The cash left

What does total value locked mean in DeFi lending? It is the USD value of supplied assets still in the protocol after borrowed funds are subtracted.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 22:44 UTC5 min read

After a lending deposit, total value locked (TVL) is the current USD value of assets held in a DeFi lending protocol’s contracts, usually counted as supplied value minus borrowed value.

That subtraction matters because borrowed tokens have left the lending contracts. Counting both the original deposit and the borrowed funds can make the same capital appear more than once, especially when a borrower supplies borrowed tokens back into a market.

A lending market holds reserves supplied by users, then lets users borrow listed assets against collateral. SparkLend is one example of a permissionless money market with that structure. Its supplied and borrowed balances make the accounting concrete: SparkLend lets users supply assets and borrow other listed assets against that collateral.

Lending TVL tracks reserves left after borrowing

The basic calculation is: TVL = supplied USD value − borrowed USD value. Each asset balance is valued at current token prices, then the borrowed value is subtracted from the supplied value. The result estimates the assets still sitting in the protocol’s reserves.

Take an illustrative market with $100 million supplied and $70 million borrowed. Its lending TVL is $30 million. That remainder is also the market’s idle liquidity: the assets available to withdraw or borrow, subject to the protocol’s market rules and available reserves.

The three figures answer different questions. Total supplied is the value users have deposited. Total borrowed is the value borrowers have drawn out. TVL, when calculated as deposits minus borrows, is the net value remaining in the contracts. A tracker may display these measures alongside one another, but they are not interchangeable.

DefiLlama excludes borrowed amounts from lending TVL by default and offers a toggle to include them. Including borrow balances can be useful when comparing gross activity or exposure, but it produces a different total. When comparing protocols, check the tracker’s method before treating two headline figures as equivalent.

Prices can move the figure even when users do nothing. Suppose ETH’s price falls 20% while an ETH-heavy market’s deposits and borrows stay unchanged. The USD value of its ETH reserves also falls by roughly 20%, so its TVL can decline without a withdrawal. Stablecoin-heavy markets may respond differently because their reserve mix differs.

Utilization adds another useful view. It describes how much of a market’s supplied assets are borrowed, so it helps explain why a market with substantial deposits may have little immediately available liquidity. TVL gives the net dollar amount; utilization gives a ratio of borrowing against supply. Read together, they describe the reserve and how much of it is in use.

Looping raises gross totals without adding new capital

Looping is a common source of inflated supply figures. A user supplies collateral, borrows against it, and supplies some of the borrowed funds back into the market. The same initial capital now supports a larger gross supplied balance, while the borrowed balance rises too.

In an illustrative loop, a user supplies $1,000, borrows $600, then supplies that $600. The market’s total supplied rises by $600, but its borrowed total rises by the same amount. Deposits-minus-borrows TVL strips out that added borrowed amount, so the loop does not appear as $600 of new net capital in the reserve figure.

Before the loop, the market held the original deposit and had no new borrowing from this position. Afterward, gross supplied and borrowed totals both increased, while the borrower still owes the borrowed amount. The market has more assets recorded on the supply side, but those assets came from a loan already counted on the borrowing side. That is why headline “deposits” can overstate how much independent capital entered the market.

For analysts, the distinction changes the comparison. A protocol with high gross supply may have attracted substantial deposits, but some of that total can reflect repeated use of borrowed funds. Net TVL reduces this particular distortion. It still moves with prices and depends on what the tracker includes, so it is a useful accounting measure rather than a complete account of market activity.

Related

What is the difference between TVL and total supplied?

Total supplied is the gross USD value deposited into a lending market; TVL usually subtracts the value already borrowed. In a market with $100 million supplied and $70 million borrowed, those figures are $100 million and $30 million respectively. Total supplied helps show the scale of deposits, while net TVL estimates the assets remaining in the contracts.

Does a high TVL mean a lending protocol is safe?

A high TVL means a large net dollar value is recorded in the protocol under the tracker’s method. It does not by itself establish that a protocol is safe. Read the figure alongside the reserve mix, borrowing and utilization levels, and the way the protocol manages collateral and withdrawals. When comparing TVL over time, watch both token prices and changes in deposits and borrows: either can move the headline number.
