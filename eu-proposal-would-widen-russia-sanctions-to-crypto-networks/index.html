EU proposal would widen Russia sanctions to crypto networks

The Commission’s 22nd sanctions proposal targets Russian banks and crypto networks, including third-country enablers, but needs approval from all 27 EU states.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 27, 2026 · 08:23 UTC3 min read

The European Commission has proposed extending Russia sanctions to crypto and payment networks that help Moscow evade restrictions, putting third-country providers within scope as well as Russian targets. The measures are part of the EU’s proposed 22nd sanctions package, which still requires approval from all 27 member states. The Associated Press’s report on the proposal says the package could take weeks to clear and may change during that process.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the EU would focus on crypto and payment networks used to circumvent sanctions, including providers in third countries. The Commission also announced a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the effort and coordinate with international partners. The statement does not name particular platforms or specify the proposed restrictions on individual crypto services.

The change extends pressure beyond direct Russian financial institutions. In the Commission’s account, the aim is to disrupt routes that can move payments around existing restrictions, including networks outside Russia that help with evasion. That approach may broaden enforcement’s reach, while making cooperation with third countries important to its practical effect.

The financial measures sit alongside a proposed transaction ban on about 60 Russian banks, all of which would also be removed from SWIFT, the international payments messaging system. The package also proposes listing more than 70 vessels linked to Russia’s shadow fleet and banning Russian teams and athletes from competing under their national flag at EU sports events. These are proposals, not measures already in force.

The crypto provisions build on years of EU sanctions against people and organizations connected to Russia’s war in Ukraine. AP reports that the bloc has already targeted more than 4,700 people and entities. The Commission’s latest proposal shifts attention toward the channels used to get around restrictions, alongside the banks and other organizations directly targeted.

A useful comparison is the EU’s previous package, adopted in July. The Council said that round imposed asset freezes and funding prohibitions on 94 banks and financial institutions, extended transaction bans to 33 additional Russian financial institutions, and targeted 14 crypto-related service platforms. It also created the possibility of a full third-country ban on crypto-asset services used by Russia. The new proposal adds a further focus on crypto and payment networks and launches a task force, but the Commission has not yet set out specific platform names or detailed implementation rules in its public statement.

The trade-off is between widening the net around sanctions evasion and getting measures adopted and enforced across jurisdictions. The Commission says it wants to align efforts with international partners; the proposal’s reach will depend on the final text and how member states apply it. The Council’s July package shows that the EU has already used transaction bans against crypto services and providers outside Russia, but the latest statement does not establish how far the new provisions would go beyond that framework.

Next, the signals to watch are whether all 27 governments agree, what changes during negotiations, and whether the final measures identify specific crypto or payment providers. The task force’s remit and any arrangements with third countries will also indicate how the EU intends to turn the proposed crackdown into enforceable restrictions.
