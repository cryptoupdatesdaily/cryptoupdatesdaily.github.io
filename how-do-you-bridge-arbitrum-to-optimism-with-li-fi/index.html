How do you bridge Arbitrum to Optimism with LI.FI?

Bridge Arbitrum to Optimism with lifi bridge by comparing routes, signing on Arbitrum and checking delivery on OP Mainnet, usually in minutes.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 05:24 UTC4 min read

The lifi bridge routes ETH or an ERC-20 from Arbitrum One to OP Mainnet through cross-chain liquidity, usually in minutes; the canonical route through Ethereum takes about a week. For someone who has used a bridge directly, the change is the route choice: a bridge aggregator compares available ways to reach Optimism. The lifi bridge is a concrete example of how route comparison can surface liquidity bridges that fill on the destination chain.

Why use a liquidity route between Arbitrum and Optimism?

Arbitrum One and OP Mainnet do not have a direct canonical bridge between them. The canonical route first withdraws from Arbitrum to Ethereum, waits through Arbitrum’s roughly week-long challenge period, then deposits from Ethereum to Optimism. That path is useful when you specifically want the canonical route, but the wait is material when your goal is to use funds on OP Mainnet soon.

A LI.FI bridge aggregator can compare bridges that deliver on Optimism using liquidity already available there. Those routes skip the Ethereum withdrawal wait by having a bridge fill the transfer on OP Mainnet. The trade-off is that you are using a liquidity bridge rather than taking the canonical route through Ethereum. For a transfer where arrival time matters, compare the amount you will receive and the estimated time shown for each available route.

Across and Stargate are examples of bridge names that may appear in cross-chain routing. The best option can change between quotes as route availability and the amount delivered vary. Choose based on the current quote’s amount received and time, rather than assuming one bridge name is always the best choice.

What you need before starting

Have the funds you want to move on Arbitrum One, a connected wallet, and some ETH on Arbitrum for gas. OP Mainnet also uses ETH for gas, so you will need ETH there if you plan to make transactions after arrival.

For ETH, the transfer generally needs one signature on Arbitrum. For an ERC-20 such as USDC, the token may first need an approval transaction, followed by the transfer signature. Both Arbitrum transactions use Arbitrum ETH for gas. Keep that distinction in mind when comparing the quoted route and the amount you intend to send.

How to use LI.FI for bridging

  1. Set the source to Arbitrum and the destination to Optimism. Select Arbitrum One as the source chain and OP Mainnet as the destination. Confirm both before entering the transfer, since the route and destination transaction depend on those choices.
  2. Choose the token and amount. Select ETH or the ERC-20 you want to move, then enter the amount. Make sure the wallet holds that token on Arbitrum and enough Arbitrum ETH to pay gas.
  3. Compare the available routes. Look at the amount received on OP Mainnet and the estimated delivery time. The route with the largest displayed amount may take longer, while a faster quote may deliver less. Compare the current quotes rather than choosing by bridge name: the top route can change between searches.
  4. Approve the token if asked. For an ERC-20 transfer, sign the approval transaction before the transfer when the wallet requests it. Approval is a separate on-chain action and uses Arbitrum ETH for gas. ETH transfers do not need an ERC-20 token approval.
  5. Sign the transfer on Arbitrum. Review the transfer details in your wallet and confirm the transaction. This authorizes the selected route to start the cross-chain transfer; the destination delivery still has to complete.
  6. Watch the status in LI.FI Explorer and confirm arrival. Follow the transfer until the destination transaction is available. The task is complete when that transaction is on OP Mainnet and the token balance appears in your wallet there.

What to watch after signing

A submitted Arbitrum transaction means the transfer has started, not that the funds have arrived. Use the transfer status and destination transaction hash to distinguish a pending delivery from a completed one. Once the destination transaction is recorded, check the wallet on OP Mainnet and confirm the expected asset balance.

For a one-off transfer, the practical decision is whether the quoted amount and delivery time suit your needs compared with the canonical Ethereum route. Review those details before signing, then use the destination transaction and wallet balance as the signal that your funds are ready on Optimism.
