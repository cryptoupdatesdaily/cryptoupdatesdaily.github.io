US Wins Court Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Stolen Crypto

The DOJ says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, part of a wider freeze that still faces forfeiture proceedings.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 13, 2026 · 11:58 UTC3 min read

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday, November 12. The Justice Department announcement describes the amount as part of a broader pool of stolen assets frozen through government and private-sector efforts; it does not say the funds have been returned to Binance or its customers.

The distinction matters. The FBI described the action as a seizure, but the DOJ’s account says the department has obtained court authorization to “seize or restrain” the assets. That legal step gives the government authority over a portion of the frozen funds, while any ultimate transfer of ownership remains subject to forfeiture proceedings. The Associated Press reports that a judge typically determines who has a legitimate claim to assets in such cases.

$1.2 billion authorized within a $2.7 billion freeze

The DOJ says more than $2.7 billion of the stolen cryptocurrency has been frozen so far through combined US government and private-sector efforts. The amount covered by court authorization, more than $1.2 billion, is therefore a subset of that total, not an additional recovery. For Binance users following the case, the figures describe assets made harder to move and a legal route toward forfeiture; they do not establish how much will ultimately be recovered or when.

The scale begins with the theft. The DOJ says that on or about October 29, North Korean military members, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The AP puts the theft at about $6.3 billion. Against that reported loss, the $2.7 billion freeze represents a substantial interruption of the stolen funds’ movement, while the newly authorized amount is a narrower legal step within it.

Tracing and forfeiture remain open

The government says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the remaining assets are ongoing. According to the DOJ, TraderTraitor actors continue to launder cryptocurrency through bridges, mixers and exchanges. The FBI has urged virtual-asset service providers to block transactions involving addresses used to move or launder the Binance assets. That work means the amount frozen can change, but the announcement gives no final recovery total or timetable for proceedings.

Freezing crypto can prevent it from being transferred while investigators and service providers coordinate, but a freeze is not the same as a completed forfeiture. The court authorization advances the process for more than $1.2 billion of the frozen assets; it does not settle who owns those assets or how they may ultimately be distributed. The AP’s account says a judge typically considers competing legitimate claims in forfeiture cases.

The DOJ also announced a separate disruption of cloud infrastructure tied to FaceStealer, a North Korean social-engineering scheme it says has stolen more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the past 18 months. That action is distinct from the Binance case, and the announcement does not connect FaceStealer to the Binance breach. The separate operation shows the scope of Thursday’s announcement, but it does not change the status of Binance-related funds.

The next concrete signals are whether authorities obtain further seizure or forfeiture orders, whether service providers freeze additional assets as tracing continues, and whether a court determines claims to the funds already under authorization. Until then, the confirmed figures mark progress in restricting and pursuing the stolen crypto, not a final accounting of what Binance or its users will recover.
