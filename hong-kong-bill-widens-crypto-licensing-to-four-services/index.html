Hong Kong bill widens crypto licensing to four services

Hong Kong has gazetted a bill requiring crypto dealers, advisers, managers and custodians to register or obtain SFC licences, widening oversight beyond exchanges.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 04:55 UTC3 min read

Hong Kong has gazetted a bill that would extend crypto licensing to dealing, advisory, management and custody services, bringing businesses beyond trading platforms into the Securities and Futures Commission’s (SFC) regulatory perimeter. The government’s announcement on the Virtual Asset Services Legislation (Amendment) Bill 2026 says providers operating these services as a business in Hong Kong would need an SFC licence or registration and would face fit-and-proper tests and other regulatory requirements. The bill is scheduled for its first reading in the Legislative Council on December 2; it is a proposal, not yet law.

The change broadens the framework from oversight of crypto trading platforms to a wider set of functions around digital assets. The SFC already licenses virtual asset trading platforms, while Hong Kong’s stablecoin issuer regime took effect in August 2025, according to CoinDesk’s account of the bill. The new measure would add firms that deal in assets for clients, advise on them, manage portfolios or safeguard assets. That makes the framework more closely resemble conventional financial regulation in its coverage of activities across the investment process, though each regime’s precise rules will determine how that comparison works in practice.

The proposed requirements would also apply to banks and stored-value facility operators regulated by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority if they want to provide any of the covered virtual asset services. They would have to register with the SFC, while the bill would give both the SFC and the HKMA powers to implement the regime. The government says the framework draws on international anti-money-laundering and investor-protection standards while taking account of local conditions and aligning with rules for conventional financial services.

The proposal follows separate consultations on the parts of this expanded framework. In May, the SFC and the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau said they had concluded consultations on advisory and management regimes, with their scopes aligned respectively to existing Type 4 securities-advice and Type 9 asset-management activities. Their consultation conclusion described those regimes alongside the proposed dealing and custody rules as a way to cover key parts of the digital asset market. Gazetting a single amendment bill now brings those four activity categories together in a legislative proposal.

The policy trade-off is clear in the bill’s design: extending fit-and-proper checks and regulatory requirements to more service providers could strengthen oversight of advice, asset management and custody, while requiring additional firms to meet SFC standards. The government presents the expansion as both an investor-protection measure and a route to clearer compliance for businesses. Whether that balance holds will depend on the final legislation and how regulators apply the requirements across different types of provider.

The next signal is the bill’s first reading on December 2, followed by its progress through the Legislative Council. The key details to watch are the scope of each covered service, the requirements imposed on licensees and registrants, and how the SFC and HKMA divide implementation responsibilities. Until those provisions are settled and take effect, firms have a proposed framework to assess, rather than a completed licensing regime.
