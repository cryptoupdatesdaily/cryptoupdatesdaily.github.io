MoonPay seeks OCC trust charter for stablecoin operations

MoonPay has applied for a national trust bank charter to offer custody, stablecoin issuance and reserve management, pending review by the OCC.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 05:11 UTC3 min read

MoonPay has applied to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to establish a national trust bank that could provide custody and stablecoin services if approved. The application outlines a proposed expansion into issuing and redeeming stablecoins and managing their reserves, alongside fiduciary custody and currency conversion for clients.

The filing sets out a proposed trust bank in Miami Beach

MoonPay’s application, dated Nov. 17 and received by the OCC on Nov. 18, proposes MoonPay National Trust Bank in Miami Beach, Florida. The company describes it as a new, non-depository national bank whose operations would be limited to trust company business and related activities. The OCC’s public application says the bank would provide fiduciary custody; stablecoin issuance, redemption and reserve management; and conversion services as an agent.

The application marks a proposal, not an authorization to begin those activities. MoonPay says it is seeking no federal deposit insurance, and the application’s Federal Reserve status field identifies the proposed institution as a member bank. The filing also says the proposed bank would serve clients digitally, with its Miami Beach office not open to the public.

MoonPay is seeking a federal route beyond its existing footprint

The charter would give the company a proposed federal structure for the listed services. MoonPay already has a New York trust charter and offers enterprise stablecoin services, according to The Block’s report on the filing. The new application would therefore extend its regulatory footprint and seek to place custody, stablecoin functions and client conversion within one nationally chartered trust institution, subject to OCC review.

MoonPay’s application enters a field where other digital-asset firms are also seeking OCC oversight. The regulator’s pending-application list includes Galaxy National Bank, Ramp National Trust Bank, Rain National Trust Bank and several other applicants. Some firms have received conditional or final approvals, The Block reports, but MoonPay’s filing remains pending. That comparison shows the charter is part of a broader industry push for national trust-bank structures; it does not establish that MoonPay will receive approval or operate on the same terms as other applicants.

The proposed scope leaves key limits and questions

The non-depository model distinguishes the proposal from a conventional bank that takes customer deposits. MoonPay is seeking powers tied to trust-company operations, including custody and stablecoin services, rather than federal deposit insurance. That narrower scope still carries a trade-off: national reach would come through a regulated charter, while the application would have to clear the OCC’s review before the proposed bank could operate.

Details of the business plan are confidential, so the public filing does not show the planned scale, launch timetable, reserve composition or specific stablecoin arrangements. It also does not establish whether MoonPay intends to issue a new token or provide services for existing issuers. The OCC says its application list records pending filings and provides public application materials where required. Its published notice says comments may be submitted within 30 days of publication.

The next signals are whether the OCC advances the application, what conditions or requests for further information emerge, and whether the public comment period raises issues about the proposed activities. Until those steps are clearer, the filing sets out MoonPay’s intended federal structure and services, not a completed expansion.
