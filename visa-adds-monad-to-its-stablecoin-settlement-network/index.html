Visa adds Monad to its stablecoin settlement network

Visa added Monad as a stablecoin settlement option for participating issuers and acquirers, widening its network menu as institutional privacy tools remain in development.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 11, 2026 · 12:00 UTC3 min read

Visa has added the Monad blockchain as a stablecoin settlement option for participating issuers and acquirers, giving them another network for settling obligations with Visa. The Monad Foundation announcement carried by GlobeNewswire says the option is available now; it does not name an issuer or acquirer using Monad, or specify which stablecoins they can settle there.

The change widens the network choices in Visa’s existing program rather than announcing a new consumer card product. The Foundation says cardholders’ Visa experience remains familiar while settlement happens behind the scenes. It also says Visa reported a $25 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate as of September 2026, alongside more than 225 stablecoin-linked card programs globally.

How does Monad change Visa’s network options?

Monad joins nine networks Visa said it supported in April: Avalanche, Ethereum, Solana, Stellar, Arc, Base, Canton, Polygon and Tempo. In its April announcement adding five blockchains, Visa described the expansion as giving partners more choice while Visa provides a common settlement layer. Monad adds to that multi-chain approach; the announcement does not say Visa is shifting settlement away from the other networks.

The Foundation describes Monad as fully compatible with the Ethereum Virtual Machine. It says issuers and acquirers that already use Ethereum-based custody, wallet and compliance tools can typically extend those integrations to Monad. That could reduce the need to build new tooling, though the release does not identify partners that have done so or quantify integration costs.

What are the potential benefits and the open questions?

The Foundation says settlement on Monad can let funds move across weekends and holidays, when traditional card settlement has typically followed a five-business-day banking schedule. It also cites sub-second finality as a way to make settlement cycles more predictable for acquirers. Those are the announcement’s stated benefits; it gives no transaction volumes, cost comparisons or partner results specific to Visa settlement on Monad.

Privacy is another part of the pitch, but it is still in development. The Foundation says its planned Private Settlement feature will let institutions keep sensitive transaction details confidential while sharing them with auditors and regulators when needed. It says the feature is designed to keep private activity connected to the network’s wider liquidity. The announcement gives no launch date beyond “in coming months,” so institutions cannot yet assess the live feature’s controls or performance.

That planned functionality sits alongside Visa’s existing exploration of privacy on Canton. The Block reported that Visa announced a proof of concept with stablecoin issuer Brale to test settlement using its dollar-backed SBC token. Monad’s release makes no direct comparison of the two approaches. For institutions weighing open network access against confidentiality requirements, the practical difference will depend on how each system handles data access and liquidity once used in live settlement.

What should readers watch next?

The next useful signals are whether Visa names issuers or acquirers using Monad, which stablecoins they settle, and whether the planned privacy feature launches on schedule. Reported settlement volumes and partner feedback would show whether the added network becomes an active route or remains an available option. Until those details emerge, the announcement establishes expanded access, not disclosed adoption or measured performance.
