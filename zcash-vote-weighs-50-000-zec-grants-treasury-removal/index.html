Zcash vote weighs 50,000 ZEC grants-treasury removal

Zcash’s Nov. 16–30 polls will weigh a draft to remove 50,000 ZEC from grants and redirect future funding, shifting support toward miners and a locked fund.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 12, 2026 · 16:58 UTC4 min read

Zcash coinholders and community panels are due to vote Nov. 16–30 on a draft that would remove 50,000 ZEC—about $50 million at the value cited in current reporting—from the Zcash Community Grants treasury and direct its value toward future miner rewards. The proposal would also end the grants program’s automatic share of new issuance, changing how development funding is allocated while leaving existing treasury funds available for grants.

The draft ZIP merged into Zcash’s specifications repository on Nov. 11. It was written by Sean Bowe, Zooko Wilcox and Jason McGee. The draft says the 50,000 ZEC removal would take place within 30 days after the proposal’s activation in a network upgrade. Its value would be credited to the Network Sustainability Mechanism (NSM), a reserve that returns ZEC through future block rewards rather than releasing it all at once.

How would the plan change the grants program?

At activation, Zcash Community Grants (ZCG) would stop receiving its current 8% share of newly created ZEC. That same share would instead accumulate until Zcash’s third halving in a new Future Dev and Legal Defense Fund lockbox. The draft does not provide a way to spend from that fund; a separate proposal would need to establish withdrawal rules.

ZCG would retain its remaining treasury and continue making grants. CoinDesk reports that a draft-cited October snapshot put the treasury at 91,453 ZEC, making the proposed removal about 55% of that balance. The draft’s estimate, using a ZEC price assumption of $1,300 and spending from the first half of 2026, puts the remaining treasury at roughly six years of expenses after the transfer, compared with about 13 years before it. Those are estimates based on stated assumptions, not a fixed funding runway.

The separate 12% Coinholder-Controlled Fund would remain unchanged, according to CoinDesk. That leaves a continuing route for coinholders to direct some development funding even as the automatic ZCG stream ends. The trade-off is that ZCG would have less future income and a smaller reserve, while future development and legal work would receive a new allocation whose spending rules remain undecided.

How does this compare with the earlier proposal?

The current draft follows a broader ZCG plan to redirect about 98,000 ZEC from existing assets and future income toward mining rewards. CoinDesk reported that ZCG dropped that plan on Nov. 3 after criticism. The replacement narrows the proposed treasury removal to 50,000 ZEC and keeps ZCG operating on its remaining funds, but it still shifts value away from grant capacity and toward the NSM.

The alternatives are not limited to this draft. In its polling timeline, Zcash community organizers said several proposals would be considered and that retaining the existing funding arrangements unchanged should be an explicit poll option. They described the poll as a way to gauge rough consensus, with an upgrade called NU7.1 to follow if support emerges. That makes the vote a choice among competing funding paths, rather than a binding transfer taking place immediately.

What happens after the polls close?

The polls close Nov. 30 at 11:59 p.m. UTC. Organizers say they will publish results shortly afterward and assess whether there is rough consensus. If so, engineering teams and miners would work toward an NU7.1 upgrade and agree on a target date. CoinDesk reports that the draft has no activation height or reference implementation yet, so the proposal’s passage would not by itself set when the funds move.

The next signals are the poll results, whether organizers judge support broad enough to proceed, and whether the follow-up upgrade defines a target date and implementation. The separate question of how the new lockbox could fund development and legal defense also remains open.
