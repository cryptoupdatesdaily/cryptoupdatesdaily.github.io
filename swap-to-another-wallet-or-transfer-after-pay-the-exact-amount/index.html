Swap to another wallet or transfer after: pay the exact amount

Swap to another wallet or swap then transfer to pay someone? Use a buy order with the recipient as receiver for an exact amount; transfer after when sender identity matters.

By Blockchain Today EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 09:52 UTC5 min read

For an exact payment in a different token, use a CoW Swap buy order with the recipient set as receiver; the order specifies the bought-token amount, while a sell order leaves that amount dependent on the fill. This applies when the order settles as a completed buy: the recipient gets the bought tokens directly, but the transfer comes from GPv2Settlement rather than your address.

Use this checklist before signing, especially when an invoice amount or the visible sender matters:

  • Confirm the order type and amount. A buy order targets the amount of tokens the receiver should get; a sell order specifies what you spend, so the amount delivered can vary with the fill. This matters when the invoice is denominated in the token being paid.
  • Confirm the receiver address. The receiver is separate from the signer. Check that the recipient’s address is set as receiver before signing, since the bought tokens are transferred there by GPv2Settlement.
  • Ask what sender record the payee expects. With a custom receiver, the payee sees the settlement contract as the source of the token transfer, not your wallet. If they require a transfer from your own address, swap first and then send.
  • Compare the extra transaction with the payment requirement. A separate transfer costs another gas-paid transaction, but gives you a chance to confirm the received amount and creates a transfer from your address.

A buy order is the direct choice for an exact amount

A CoW Swap buy order with the recipient as receiver is the direct choice when the payee needs a defined number of tokens and does not require the transfer to come from your wallet. The order is built around the amount to buy, so the payment amount is specified before execution rather than being whatever remains after selling a fixed input.

That distinction matters for an invoice. Suppose the counterparty asks for 250 units of a token. With a buy order, you set that bought-token amount and use the recipient’s address as receiver. The amount you spend to obtain it can depend on the trade, but the requested buy amount is the target delivered when the order settles successfully.

A sell order reverses the certainty. It fixes the amount of the token you sell, and the recipient receives the amount that the fill yields in the other token. That result can land above or below the invoice amount as the trade executes. A sell order can suit a payment where the input budget is fixed and the resulting amount is acceptable; it is a poor fit when the invoice requires an exact output.

CoW Swap uses signed trade intents and solver-run batch auctions. The receiver field lets the bought tokens go to the payee as part of the swap settlement, rather than requiring you to receive them first and initiate a second transfer. A gasless swap on cowswap is a useful case to understand this route: the signed intent names the receiver, and that receiver detail determines where the bought tokens go.

The settlement contract explains the unfamiliar sender

When the recipient is the custom receiver, GPv2Settlement transfers the bought tokens to that wallet. The recipient’s token activity therefore shows the settlement contract as the transfer source, rather than your signing address.

That can make a completed payment look unfamiliar to a payee who expects your wallet address to appear as sender. The transaction may have reached the right wallet and still fail their usual recognition check, especially if they identify payments by matching the transfer source to a known payer address. The receiver field controls the destination; it does not make your wallet the token transfer’s source.

Send the order ID to the recipient as proof of payment. They can use it to locate the order in CoW Explorer and connect the swap to the arrival in their wallet. Include the order ID with the invoice reference or payment note so the recipient can match the trade to the amount they expected. If the recipient cannot find the transfer, compare the receiver address and the order’s settlement status before treating the payment as complete.

A separate transfer suits payments that need your address

Swap first and transfer after when the payee must see a token transfer from your own address, or when you want to inspect the result before sending. The flow is two transactions: complete the swap to your wallet, then send the chosen amount to the counterparty.

This approach gives you a clean sender record for the payment transfer. It also lets you confirm the amount of tokens in your wallet before choosing what to send. That can help when the obligation is exact but the swap output itself needs checking before you make the payment. The trade-off is an additional gas-paid transaction for the separate send.

The two-step route also separates the swap from the payment. The recipient sees a transfer from your address, while the swap remains an earlier transaction into your wallet. That separation may fit the counterparty’s reconciliation process, but it means the recipient’s transfer record alone does not show how you obtained the tokens.

Choose by amount, sender record, and transaction cost

Use a buy order with the recipient as receiver when the amount must match the invoice and the payee can identify settlement through the order ID. Use swap then transfer when the payee requires a transfer from your own address or you need to verify the acquired amount before sending.

Before signing, compare the exact output required, the receiver address, and the sender address the payee expects to see. Then share the order ID for a direct receiver swap, or the separate transfer record for a two-step payment. Those details explain both what arrived and why the sender may look different from the payer.
